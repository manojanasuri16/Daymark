# Daymark Privacy Policy

**Effective date: 2026-11-08**

Daymark is a private, offline-first habit recovery and streak tracking app. This policy explains, plainly, what the app does and does not do with your information.

## Summary

Daymark does not collect, transmit or share any personal data. Everything you enter stays on your device.

## Data we collect

None. Daymark has no account system, no analytics, no advertising and no crash-reporting service. We (the developer) never receive your streak dates, slip entries, moods, triggers, notes or settings.

## Data stored on your device

The information you enter (trackers, slip logs, urges you rode out, journal entries and any photos you attach to them, your reasons, cost estimates, moods, triggers, situations, notes and app settings) is stored locally on your device only. It is not sent anywhere. The same goes for the events you create (names, dates, notes, colours and fonts) and their settings.

## No internet access

Daymark does not request the Android INTERNET permission. The app is technically unable to send data over the network.

## Calendar (optional)

Daymark can import upcoming events from your phone's calendar so you can count down to them. This is entirely optional: the app asks for Android's calendar read permission (READ_CALENDAR) only when you tap "Import from calendar", after explaining why. Daymark reads your calendar once, on this device, to show you a list. Only the events you tick are copied into Daymark as countdowns (title and date); the rest is discarded. Nothing is synced, nothing is written back to your calendar, and nothing leaves your phone. You can decline, or revoke the permission in Android settings at any time, and the rest of the app works the same.

## Notifications

If you turn on reminders, Daymark asks for Android's notification permission and shows local notifications (a daily check-in, milestone celebrations, habit reminders and, if you switch them on, event alerts). They are scheduled on your device; nothing is sent to a server. By default ("discreet mode") notifications, widgets, the Quick Settings tile and share images never name what you are tracking.

## Journal photos

If you attach a photo to a journal entry, you pick it with Android's photo picker, which gives Daymark access only to the photos you select. Daymark stores a resized copy in its own private storage. The copy is not added to your gallery, and re-encoding it removes camera metadata such as location. Photos are included in backups unless you turn that off in Settings, and are deleted when you delete the entry or erase all data.

## Sharing

If you share a milestone, Daymark draws the image on your device and hands it to the app you pick in Android's share sheet. Daymark itself sends nothing; what happens next is up to the app you chose.

## Biometric and device-credential unlock

Daymark offers an optional app lock. Unlocking is handled entirely by the Android system (BiometricPrompt). The app only learns whether authentication succeeded. It never receives, stores or transmits your fingerprint, face data or PIN.

## Backups

You can export your data to a file (habits, events and settings) and import it later, optionally protected with a password (the file is then encrypted with AES-256-GCM; the password is never stored with it). If you turn on automatic backups, Daymark saves a copy once a day into a folder you pick and keeps the last seven; if you set a password for them, it is stored only on your device so the app can encrypt each copy. You decide where backups are saved and who can access them. Daymark does not upload backups, though a folder you choose may itself be synced by another app. Android cloud backup (auto-backup) is disabled for the app, so your data is not copied to Google Drive or other cloud services by the system on our behalf.

## Deleting your data

You are in control. Using "Erase all data" in the app, or uninstalling the app, removes all data Daymark stores on your device. Backup files you exported yourself are separate; delete them from wherever you saved them.

## Children's privacy

Daymark does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children under 13 (or the applicable age in your region). Because the app collects no data, there is nothing to collect from children.

## Not medical advice

Daymark is a self-tracking tool and is not a substitute for professional medical or mental health care.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this location with a new effective date. Because the app collects no data, we do not expect changes that affect your privacy.
